Five women, one shop

Clara Della Femina and Margaret Griffin opened the beauty parlor at 46 South Street in the mid-1930s. Clara’s father was a barber with his own shop down the block at 34 South Street, and when she finished beauty school he set her up with a shop of her own.

In the mid-1960s Mary Ellen Shippey and Kathleen Byrda bought it. Decades later Kathleen was ready to let it go, struggled to find a buyer, and threw a closing party for her regulars.

Then Laura Dembski walked in on a lunch break, and by the end of that week the shop was hers. Kathleen had one condition: keep the standing appointments. Laura kept them, and opened as Miss Laura’s on April Fool’s Day, 1998.

She restored the old parlor toward its vintage look, mixing old and new, because she wanted it to feel like the beauty parlors of old: a neighborhood shop for people of every background.

“I run the oldest continuously running beauty parlor in Jamaica Plain, possibly Boston!”

Laura Dembski, to Boston Voyager, 2018
