Life OS

A dashboard for my whole day that opens when I log in, and reads it to me through Siri in the morning.

Built in 2026 for myself, and I use it every day. The screenshot and the sample briefing use made-up data.

What it does

My day
My classes, the gym split and calendar events on a timeline with a line for now and the free gaps in between.
Tasks
Read from and written to my Obsidian notes. Typing “lab report friday” adds a task due Friday.
Canvas
Every assignment and quiz due in the next two weeks, with a reminder before each one.
Email
Unread Gmail sorted into Needs you, Worth a look and Later, with the reasons for each email’s place.
Habits and focus
A focus timer that logs study sessions and keeps a streak going.
Weather and news
The week’s forecast, and headlines from Boston, Northeastern, tech and golf.

The morning briefing

I say “Hey Siri, Hello Life OS” and my phone reads me my day. This is the same briefing with sample classes, email and tasks. The weather and headlines are live.

Good morning, Kai. It’s Thursday, November 5, 8:05 AM.

In Boston, it’s 52 degrees and partly cloudy, with a high of 61 and a low of 45.

You have 2 things on your calendar: Calculus at 9:15 AM and Physics lab at 11:15 AM. The first one starts in 1 hour and 10 minutes.

At the gym, it’s anterior day.

On Canvas, Lab 4 report is due tomorrow at 11:59 PM and Problem set 7 is due on Saturday at 11:59 AM.

You have 3 open tasks. Due today: Email the co-op office back.

In your inbox, one email needs you: Professor Smith about the Lab 4 deadline.

Your Study streak is at 4 days. One session today keeps it going.

That’s your briefing. Have a good one.

How it works

Sources

Gmail over IMAP, the Canvas API, my class sections from Northeastern’s class search, my Obsidian vault, Open-Meteo weather, RSS news

→

My PC

A small Node.js server, started at login. It only answers this computer and keeps every password on it.

→

Dashboard

Opens in its own Chrome window when I log in

Every 5 minutes

The PC uploads my tasks, habits and class schedule

→

Vercel

A serverless function and private storage. It fetches mail, Canvas, weather and news itself, and writes the briefing.

→

Siri

A Shortcut fetches the text and speaks it

Problems I had to solve

45,000 unread emails

Gmail’s own “important” label wasn’t enough. Each email gets a score: up for people I’ve written to, Northeastern addresses and words like “deadline” or “interview”, down for promotions, no-reply senders and newsletters. Only the last 14 days are sorted, and each panel loads on its own, so a slow inbox doesn’t hold up the rest.

A briefing with the computer off

At first the phone had to reach my PC, which only worked on home Wi-Fi with the PC on. Now the briefing runs on Vercel. My tasks only live on the PC, so it uploads them while it’s on, and the cloud works out today’s classes and gym day by itself. A cloud function runs on UTC, so the times were five hours off until I set it to Boston time.

My class schedule

Canvas knows my courses but not when they meet. Life OS takes the section numbers from Canvas, looks up the meeting times and rooms in Northeastern’s public class search, and turns them into a weekly schedule with finals.

Keeping it private

The PC server only answers this computer. The cloud briefing needs a private key, the passwords are encrypted secrets, and the uploaded tasks go to private storage. The demo mode on this page swaps in made-up data, so it can be shown without showing my life.

Built with

Node.js with no web framework, plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, Vercel Functions and Blob storage, ImapFlow, node-ical, Siri Shortcuts and the Web Speech API.
